================================================================================

Goal: For each fold-switching pair, assign fold preference (F1/F2/Amb) to
each MSA cluster from AF2 TM-scores, then run ancestral state reconstruction
on the coarse cluster-level tree to infer evolutionary transitions.

User decisions:
  - Cluster-level analysis (coarse tree, one leaf per cluster)
  - delta = 0.05 for F1/F2/Amb threshold
  - Amb as third state (conservative)
  - AF2 only; simple post-hoc ESM concordance check
  - Use existing UPGMA trees (no FastTree rebuild)
  - No Tier 3 (no ancestral sequence reconstruction)

PRIORITY 1: CLASSIFY PAIRS BY FOLD-SWITCH TRIGGER TYPE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[ANA-1] DONE (2026) - Classify 93 fold-switching pairs by trigger mechanism
  Implemented in scripts/classify_triggers_from_pdb.py (auto from PDB metadata,
  no manual curation needed). Output docs/triggers_from_pdb.csv. Distribution:
  29 ligand / 19 oligomerization / 17 protein_binding / 15 equilibrium_or_unknown
  / 13 mutation. Stratified concordance by trigger + class-level tests followed.
  Original plan retained below for context.

  Impact:  HIGH — stratifying results by trigger type could explain WHY
    86% of pairs show no cluster diversity (many may be ligand-triggered
    where the alternative fold isn't encoded in co-evolutionary patterns).
    If equilibrium switchers show higher diversity than triggered switchers,
    that's a clean mechanistic story for the paper.

  Effort:  MED — primarily literature curation, not coding.
    a) Check if Porter/Looger 2018 (PNAS) supplementary tables already
       classify trigger types (their Table S1 had 96 proteins).
    b) Check Chakravarty & Porter 2023 "Distinguishing features" (Table 1
       or supplements) for trigger classification.
    c) If not available: manual literature curation for 94 pairs (~2-3 days).
       For each pair, look up the original characterization paper and note:
       - Equilibrium (both folds populated spontaneously)
       - Binding partner (fold switch upon protein-protein interaction)
       - Ligand (small molecule triggers switch)
       - pH / temperature / redox / oligomerization
       - Unknown / not characterized
    d) Add trigger_type column to data/foldswitch_PDB_IDs_full.txt or
       a new data/fold_switch_metadata.csv.
    e) Stratify fold_diversity_survey.py results by trigger type.

  Risk:    LOW — even if trigger info is incomplete for some pairs, the
    stratification adds value. The classification itself is a contribution.

  Expected outcome:
    Equilibrium switchers: higher diversity (co-evolution encodes both folds)
    Triggered switchers: lower diversity (alternative fold is environmentally
    induced, not sequence-encoded)

  Where trigger info lives:
    - NOT in PDB files (no standard annotation for fold-switch mechanism)
    - NOT in Table_S1B_final.xlsx (only structural metrics: TM, RMSD, SeqID)
    - Scattered across individual characterization papers for each protein
    - Possibly in Porter group supplementary data (check first)

  Proxy approach (faster, less reliable):
    Compare ligands in fold1 vs fold2 PDB structures. If one PDB has a
    ligand/binding partner that the other doesn't, flag as likely triggered.
    Automatable via PDB REST API, but noisy.

NOTES ON FOLD-SWITCH TRIGGER TYPES (background for ANA-1)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The field classifies fold-switching into two broad categories
  (Chakravarty & Porter, Protein Science 2023):

  1. EQUILIBRIUM SWITCHING
     Both folds populated simultaneously under physiological conditions.
     Examples: lymphotactin XCL1 (~50/50 at 37C), some NusG family members.
     Expected: dual-fold co-evolutionary signal should be strong.
     Our prediction: HIGHER cluster diversity (co-evolution encodes both).

  2. TRIGGERED SWITCHING
     External stimulus shifts population from one fold to the other:
     a) Binding partner: RfaH + RNAP, Orf9b + TOM70, Mad2 + partner
     b) Ligand: KaiB (ATP/ADP), small molecules
     c) pH: hemagglutinin (endosomal acidification)
     d) Temperature: lymphotactin (also equilibrium), designed systems
     e) Redox: CLIC1 (chloride intracellular channel)
     f) Oligomerization: concentration-dependent (KaiB tetramer)
     g) Membrane insertion: pore-forming toxins
     h) Proteolytic cleavage: serpins (irreversible)
     Expected: alternative fold may not be encoded in sequence co-evolution.
     Our prediction: LOWER cluster diversity.

  Why this matters for interpretation:
  - If triggered switchers dominate the 86% non-diverse pairs, the low
    diversity rate is explained: AF2/MSAT can't detect folds that are
    environmentally induced rather than sequence-encoded.
  - If equilibrium switchers also show low diversity, it means the
    co-evolutionary signal is interleaved within clusters (Porter's
    finding at the family level holds at the cluster level too).

  - Table_S1B_final.xlsx: NO trigger info (only TM/RMSD/SeqID)
