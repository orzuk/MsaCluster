Clade-level fold preference in fold-switching proteins

Per-cluster predictions and statistics for 93 fold-switching pairs. Companion site to Abecassis & Zuk.

Each fold-switching pair has a deep multiple sequence alignment partitioned into 20 to 100 tree-guided clusters. Every cluster is scored by seven methods for its preference between the pair's two experimental folds, and the per-cluster values feed the concordance, phylogenetic and clade-shift tests.

Methods scored per cluster

AlphaFold2, AlphaFold3, ESMFold2, Boltz-2, BioEmu, ThermoMPNN-ΔΔG and S4PRED. Two contact predictors, MSA-Transformer and CCMpred, are reported for reference and enter no analysis: a single cluster's alignment is too shallow for direct-coupling analysis, and CCMpred returns exactly zero on 71% of clusters.

How to read the tables

  • Clusters are not clades. They are groups of related sequences cut from a sequence tree, and only 12% are monophyletic, so a cluster can pool sequences from several parts of the tree.
  • A significant Moran's I is not by itself fold-specific. Generic sequence traits of the same clusters (gap fraction, identity to the query, net charge, hydrophobicity) are phylogenetically autocorrelated as often or more often.
  • Concordance measures reproducibility across methods, not independent biological confirmation: five of the seven methods take the same cluster medoid as input.
