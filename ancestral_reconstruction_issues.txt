ANCESTRAL FOLD-PREFERENCE RECONSTRUCTION — ISSUES & ANALYSIS
Created: 2026-02-23
================================================================================

This document evaluates the Phase 10 ancestral reconstruction plan, its existing
implementation, preliminary results, and scientific concerns — particularly in
light of recent literature (Porter, Schafer et al. 2024-2025) on whether AF2
actually uses MSA co-evolutionary signal or memorizes training structures.

================================================================================
1. PLAN SUMMARY
================================================================================

Goal: For each of 93 fold-switching pairs, label MSA clusters with fold
preference (F1/F2/Amb) from AF2 TM-scores, then run ancestral state
reconstruction on a coarse cluster-level tree to infer where evolutionary
fold transitions occurred.

Design decisions:
  - Cluster-level analysis (one leaf per cluster, ~5-25 leaves per pair)
  - delta = 0.05 TM-score threshold for F1/F2/Amb
  - Amb as third state (conservative)
  - AF2 only for fold assignment; post-hoc ESM concordance
  - Existing UPGMA trees (no FastTree rebuild)
  - No ancestral sequence reconstruction (Tier 3 excluded)

================================================================================
4. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS: WHY SO LITTLE FOLD DIVERSITY?
================================================================================

The "uniform" result is likely NOT because fold preference is genuinely
uniform across clusters. Rather, it reflects limitations of AF2 as a
fold-preference sensor. Three factors contribute:

A) AF2 MEMORIZATION (Porter et al. 2024, Nature Communications)
   - AF2 is a "weak predictor of fold switching" — 35% success rate
     combining >282,000 models across AF2+AF3 implementations
   - ACE method proved co-evolutionary signal for BOTH folds exists in
     56/56 fold-switching MSAs, yet AF2 misses it in 35/56 cases
   - AF2 memorizes training-set structures and associates them with
     MSA sequences rather than inferring from co-evolutionary signal
   - AF2's confidence metrics (pLDDT, pTM) actively select AGAINST
     alternative conformations

B) AF2 DOMINANCE BIAS
   - AF2 tends to predict the dominant conformation corresponding to
     the majority co-evolutionary signal in the MSA
   - Shallow MSA subsets may not have enough signal to overcome this
   - Even when clusters differ, AF2 may still default to the same fold

C) SHALLOW MSA QUALITY
   - Porter/Schafer (Nature 2025, "Sequence clustering confounds AF2"):
     AF-cluster underperforms random sequence sampling
   - Shallow clusters (≤11 seqs) used by AF-cluster are too small
     to carry meaningful co-evolutionary signal
   - Our clusters are larger (min 200 seqs), but the principle stands:
     AF2 may not respond to MSA composition changes the way we expect

D) THE THRESHOLD ISSUE
   - delta=0.05 on the max-TM per cluster may wash out real signal
   - Many clusters have TM1=0.52, TM2=0.50 — labeled Amb, but a
     consistent slight lean across many predictions might be real
   - The "max of 5 AF2 models" aggregation further reduces sensitivity

5. SCIENTIFIC LITERATURE CONTEXT
================================================================================

Key papers from Porter/Schafer group:

[1] Chakravarty et al. (2024) Nature Communications 15:7296
    "AlphaFold predictions of fold-switched conformations are driven by
    structure memorization"
    - AF2 = weak predictor, 35% success rate on training-set proteins
    - 14% success outside training set → memorization, not physics
    - ACE proves dual-fold co-evolutionary signal exists but AF2 misses it
    - AF2 confidence metrics select AGAINST alternative folds

[2] Schafer et al. (2025) Nature 638:E8-E12
    "Sequence clustering confounds AlphaFold2"
    - AF-cluster underperforms random sampling
    - Clustered MSAs not needed; alternative folds predictable from
      single sequences (when they're in training set)
    - Co-evolutionary information from sequence clusters is insufficient

[3] Schafer et al. (2025) Protein Science
    "AlphaFold2's training set powers its predictions of some
    fold-switched conformations"
    - CFold (AF2 retrained on limited dataset) fails where AF2 succeeds
    - Confirms training set memorization drives success

[4] Porter & Looger (2018) PNAS 115:5968-5973
    "Extant fold-switching proteins are widespread"
    - Estimated 0.5-4% of PDB proteins switch folds
    - Defines the dataset and scientific framework

CRITICAL INSIGHT FROM THE LITERATURE:
  The co-evolutionary signal for BOTH folds EXISTS in the MSA (proven by ACE).
  But AF2 CANNOT reliably extract it. This means:
  - Using AF2 as the "fold sensor" for ancestral reconstruction may miss
    real evolutionary transitions
  - MSA Transformer / DCA-based contact maps may be BETTER sensors of
    fold preference because they directly read co-evolutionary signal
    without the memorization confound

6. METHOD-BY-METHOD ASSESSMENT FOR FOLD-PREFERENCE DETECTION
================================================================================

A) AF2 (current approach)
   Pros: High accuracy when it works; directly outputs 3D structure
   Cons: Memorization bias; tends to predict one fold regardless of MSA;
         confidence metrics anti-correlated with alternative folds;
         35% success rate on fold switchers
   Expected outcome: Most clusters → same fold or Amb
   VERDICT: Weakest fold-preference sensor due to memorization

B) AF3
   Pros: Improved architecture; may handle some cases differently
   Cons: Same training-set memorization issue (Porter found AF3 similar
         to AF2 in their 282K-model study); likely LESS MSA-sensitive
         than AF2 because AF3 architecture deemphasizes MSA features
   Expected outcome: Even less fold diversity across clusters than AF2
   VERDICT: Not worth running specifically for ancestral reconstruction

C) ESMFold (single-sequence)
   Pros: No MSA needed — tests whether individual sequences within a
         cluster encode fold preference at the single-sequence level;
         10 samples per cluster gives a "vote" distribution; fast
   Cons: Lower accuracy (~77% vs 88%); single-sequence so no co-
         evolutionary signal at all; same memorization issues;
         Porter found ESMFold also fails on fold-switchers
   Key point: ESMFold is MSA-INDEPENDENT so it tests a different
         hypothesis — whether the sequences themselves (not the cluster's
         co-evolutionary pattern) encode fold preference
   VERDICT: Worth analyzing existing data (already run on 10 samples
         per cluster). If ESMFold shows MORE fold variation than AF2,
         that's interesting — it means sequence composition differs
         between clusters even if AF2 ignores it.
         If ESMFold shows LESS variation, it confirms the problem is
         fundamental (sequences in different clusters are too similar).

D) MSA Transformer (contact maps) — MOST PROMISING
   Pros:
   - Directly extracts co-evolutionary signal from MSA
   - ACE (which uses MSA-Trans + GREMLIN) proved the dual-fold signal
     EXISTS in fold-switching MSAs
   - No memorization confound — MSA-Trans was not trained on structures
   - Contact maps from different clusters should reflect different co-
     evolutionary patterns IF the clusters truly differ in fold bias
   - Already computed for many pairs (output_cmaps/msa_transformer/)
   Cons:
   - Outputs contact maps, not 3D structures, so "fold preference"
     must be defined via contact-map similarity to ground truth
   - Contact map comparison is noisier than TM-score
   - Need to define a clean fold-preference metric from contact maps
   Approach: For each cluster's predicted contact map, compute overlap
         with truth-fold-1 unique contacts vs truth-fold-2 unique contacts.
         A cluster whose contact map has more fold-1-unique contacts than
         fold-2-unique contacts → F1 preference (and vice versa).
   VERDICT: Strongest candidate for detecting actual co-evolutionary fold
         signal. Should be the PRIMARY method for ancestral reconstruction,
         with AF2/ESM as secondary confirmation.

E) DCA / GREMLIN (not currently in pipeline)
   Pros: Classical co-evolutionary method; interpretable; used by ACE
   Cons: Not implemented; would need new code; slower than MSA-Trans
   VERDICT: Skip — MSA Transformer captures similar signal and is already
         computed

7. RECOMMENDED APPROACH: MULTI-METHOD FOLD PREFERENCE
================================================================================

Rather than running ancestral reconstruction only on AF2 labels, compute
fold preference from MULTIPLE methods and see which gives the most signal:

Step 1: EMPIRICAL DIVERSITY SURVEY (low effort, high info)
  For each pair, across all clusters, compute:
  - AF2: max TM1, max TM2, |TM1-TM2| per cluster
  - ESM: same from df_esm.csv (already have 10 samples per cluster)
  - MSAT: from df_cmap.csv, use recall or F1 for fold1-unique vs
    fold2-unique contacts as fold-preference metric
  Output: scatter plots of within-pair fold diversity for each method.
  This tells us WHICH method shows the most fold differentiation across
  clusters BEFORE we commit to expensive ancestral reconstruction.

Step 2: CHOOSE BEST FOLD SENSOR
  Based on Step 1, pick the method(s) that show real F1/F2 differentiation.
  Likely ranking: MSAT > ESM > AF2 (hypothesis from literature).

Step 3: RUN ANCESTRAL RECONSTRUCTION WITH BEST SENSOR
  Modify ancestral_utils.py to accept fold preferences from any method
  (not just AF2). Re-run on all pairs.

Step 4: MULTI-METHOD COMPARISON
  Run ancestral reconstruction with each method's labels. Compare:
  - Which method produces the most non-uniform patterns?
  - Do the methods agree on transition locations?
  - Concordance analysis across methods.

8. CODE-LEVEL ISSUES (independent of method choice)
================================================================================

A) classify_pattern() has a gap:
   F1->Amb->F2 transitions count as ZERO fold transitions because
   count_fold_transitions() only counts direct F1<->F2 edges.
   Fix: trace through Amb nodes when detecting transitions.

B) Representative selection in build_coarse_tree():
   Picks leaves[0] (arbitrary). Should use medoid for stability.

C) UPGMA tree is not a real phylogeny:
   UPGMA assumes molecular clock. Our trees reflect clustering geometry,
   not true evolutionary history. Must acknowledge in paper.

D) Permutation test fires rarely:
   Requires both F1 and F2 clusters present. With current AF2 labels,
   most pairs are uniform → test is skipped.

E) pastml never runs (not installed), falls back to Fitch:
   Fine for small trees. Install pastml if posterior probabilities needed.
