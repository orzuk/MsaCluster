2. NEW ANALYSIS DIMENSIONS — beyond the original raw counts
================================================================================

The original analysis used absolute-threshold classification per method
(F1 if TMdiff > 0.05, F2 if TMdiff < -0.05, else Amb) and reported "diverse"
pairs as those with both F1 and F2 clusters. Two limitations of that approach
became evident, and two new methodological dimensions were added:

A. PER-PAIR CENTERED CLASSIFICATION

  Motivation: AF2 / ESM / MSAT / DDG all have systematic biases — AF2 is
  trained more on certain folds (e.g. memorizes well-characterized chemokine
  fold), ESM defaults to one fold per single-sequence prior, ThermoMPNN's
  bias_mean is dominated by the intrinsic stability differential between the
  two backbones. Under absolute thresholds, a family where ALL clusters
  systematically prefer fold-2 (because of method bias, not biology) shows
  as "no diversity" even when clusters genuinely differ in their relative
  fold-2 commitment.

  Fix: per pair, subtract the median of TMdiff over ShallowMsa clusters,
  then classify each cluster's centered TMdiff using the same delta. A
  cluster is now "F1-centered" if it leans MORE toward F1 than the typical
  cluster in this family (regardless of absolute fold preference).

  Spread/range stats are centering-invariant — they apply to both
  classifications unchanged.

  Implementation: `_apply_centered_classification` in fold_diversity_survey.py
  applied to AF2/ESM/MSAT/CCMpred/DDG outputs. Per-pair summary CSV now has
  both `n_f1` and `n_f1_centered` columns side by side.

B. CROSS-METHOD CONCORDANCE — sign-agreement of per-cluster TMdiff_centered
   across all method pairs.

  Motivation: even when no single method's centered TMdiff crosses its delta,
  the per-cluster *direction* of fold preference can be shared across
  orthogonal methods (AF2 trained on PDB, ESM single-sequence, MSAT contact
  prediction, DDG ProteinMPNN backbone scoring — different inductive biases,
  different training data). Above-chance per-cluster sign agreement across
  these is hard to dismiss as method-specific bias.

  Implementation: `compute_pair_concordance` in fold_diversity_survey.py.
  Output: docs/fold_diversity_concordance.csv with one row per pair
  containing pairwise sign-agreement % (and approximate binomial p-values)
  for AF2~ESM, AF2~MSAT, AF2~DDG, ESM~MSAT, ESM~DDG, MSAT~DDG.

  Mean concordance is centering-invariant for sign and is the cleanest
  cross-method validation signal we have.

5. XCL1 (2n54B_2hdmA) — methodological case study
================================================================================

  Lymphotactin XCL1 — known equilibrium fold-switcher with phylogenetically
  structured fold-switching capability per Dishman et al. 2021 (Science).

  Under absolute classification: NOT flagged by any method. AF2 and ESM both
  uniformly predict the chemokine fold for every cluster; MSAT recall and
  DDG bias likewise commit to one direction for every cluster.

  Under centered classification: AF2 picks up subtle inter-cluster variation
  (6F1c / 3F2c / 12Amc). NOT flagged by ESM/MSAT/DDG centered alone.

  Cross-method concordance: ~74% mean (AF2~ESM 0.74, AF2~DDG 0.79,
  ESM~DDG 0.84, all near or above significance over 18 clusters). This
  shared per-cluster pattern across orthogonal methods is hard to explain
  as method-specific training bias.

  Caveat (important, document in paper): the centered AF2 signal correlates
  monotonically with per-cluster sequence similarity to the XCL1 query.
  Bit scores: F2c clusters (most-chemokine-confident) have bit scores 76-84,
  F1c clusters (weakest commitment) have 54-75. AF2 was trained on multiple
  XCL1 chemokine PDB structures, so prediction confidence drops smoothly
  as sequence diverges from training data. Whether the centered signal is
  "fold-switching capability" (Dishman) or "training-set proximity" (method
  artifact) cannot be distinguished from the AF2 data alone — but the fact
  that ESM/DDG agree with AF2 directionally argues against a pure
  AF2-specific artifact (their training data and prediction mechanisms
  differ).

  Also notable: top MSA hits in this family are CC-chemokines (KSCC pattern)
  rather than other XCL family members. The "C"-chemokine subfamily that
  contains XCL1 has only ~2 members; the deep MSA pulls in much more
  abundant CC chemokines, all of which are pure-chemokine-fold.

  Use as: "cross-method concordance recovers known biology that absolute
  thresholds miss" methodology demonstration. Caveat about sequence
  similarity must be acknowledged.

7. METHODOLOGICAL CAVEATS — for the paper
================================================================================

A. AF2 training-set proximity bias

  AF2's prediction confidence for a fold depends on how well-represented
  that family is in PDB. Per-cluster sequence divergence from query
  systematically affects TMdiff. Centering corrects for the average bias
  per pair but not for the gradient across clusters within a pair.

  XCL1 illustrates this: bit-score-to-query and centered TMdiff are
  monotonically correlated. The same effect operates (more weakly) for
  ESM and DDG.

  Three independent corrections discussed in the codebase:
    1. Cluster-permutation null test (already in repo, commit 2b04eae)
    2. Negative-control baseline comparison (controls postprocess RUNNING)
    3. Sequence-divergence regression per pair (NOT YET IMPLEMENTED)

B. Equilibrium vs triggered fold-switchers

  XCL1 is the canonical equilibrium fold-switcher (~50/50 in vitro). The
  method asks "do MSA clusters prefer different folds?" — sensitive to
  triggered/stratified fold-switching, less sensitive to equilibrium.

  The trigger-type classification (NEXT_TASKS Phase 12 ANA-1) is the
  natural way to stratify the results in the paper.

C. Cross-method concordance is more selective than diversity counts

  Of the 13 AF2-diverse pairs in SUMMARY_RESULTS Section 2, only ~3 cross
  the 0.65 concordance threshold. Not necessarily a problem — concordance
  asks a stronger question. But the paper should report both signals.
